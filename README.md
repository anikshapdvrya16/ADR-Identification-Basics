## 1. Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities related to the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects or other drug-related problems.

An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product.

The objective of this case study is to identify a suspected adverse drug reaction by analysing patient symptoms, medication history and the timeline of drug administration. The suspected drug is evaluated using two standard causality assessment methods: the WHO-UMC scale and the Naranjo Algorithm.

## 3. Medication History

### 3.1 Amoxicillin

Amoxicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic belonging to the penicillin group. It is used to treat susceptible bacterial infections.

Known adverse reactions include gastrointestinal disturbances and hypersensitivity reactions, such as skin rash and itching.

### 3.2 Paracetamol

Paracetamol is an analgesic and antipyretic used to relieve pain and fever. It is reported as a concomitant medication in this case.

The available information does not establish whether paracetamol contributed to the reported symptoms.

## 4. Timeline Analysis

| Timeline | Event |

**Interpretation:**

The symptoms appeared approximately three days after the initiation of amoxicillin. This temporal relationship supports investigating amoxicillin as a suspected causative drug.

However, temporal association alone does not establish causality.

## 5. Identification of the Suspected ADR

**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin

**Reported reactions:** Rash, itching, nausea and dizziness.

**Clinical reasoning:**

1. Amoxicillin is associated with hypersensitivity reactions, including rash and itching.
2. Nausea is a recognised gastrointestinal adverse effect.
3. The symptoms developed after the initiation of amoxicillin.
4. Paracetamol and other possible causes must also be considered.

**Preliminary interpretation:** A suspected amoxicillin-associated adverse drug reaction, with possible hypersensitivity features.

## 6. WHO-UMC Causality Assessment

The WHO-UMC causality assessment system evaluates the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse reaction using clinical judgement and available evidence.

### WHO-UMC causality categories

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Certain | A clear causal relationship with convincing evidence |
| Probable/Likely | Reasonable time relationship and alternative causes are unlikely |
| Possible | Reasonable time relationship, but other explanations may also exist |
| Unlikely | Timing makes a causal relationship improbable |
| Conditional/Unclassified | Additional information is required |
| Unassessable/Unclassifiable | Information is insufficient or contradictory |

### Assessment of the present case

**Preliminary WHO-UMC category: POSSIBLE**

**Justification:**

- The reaction occurred after the initiation of amoxicillin.
- Rash and itching are recognised reactions associated with amoxicillin.
- The contribution of concomitant medication and other possible causes has not been excluded.
- Information regarding improvement after drug withdrawal is unavailable.

Therefore, the available information supports a preliminary classification of Possible.

## 7. Naranjo Algorithm Assessment

The Naranjo Algorithm is a structured questionnaire containing ten questions used to estimate the probability that a suspected medicine caused an adverse drug reaction.

### Naranjo scoring system

| Total score | Causality category |
|---|---|
| 9 or more | Definite |
| 5–8 | Probable |
| 1–4 | Possible |
| 0 | Doubtful |

### Preliminary assessment

| No. | Naranjo question | Answer | Score |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Are there previous conclusive reports on this reaction? | Yes | +1 |
| 2 | Did the adverse event appear after the suspected drug was administered? | Yes | +2 |
| 3 | Did the reaction improve when the drug was discontinued or a specific antagonist was administered? | Unknown | 0 |
| 4 | Did the reaction reappear upon readministration of the drug? | Unknown | 0 |
| 5 | Are there alternative causes that could have caused the reaction? | Unknown | 0 |
| 6 | Did the reaction reappear when a placebo was given? | Unknown | 0 |
| 7 | Was the drug detected in blood or other fluids in toxic concentrations? | Unknown | 0 |
| 8 | Was the reaction more severe when the dose was increased or less severe when the dose was decreased? | Unknown | 0 |
| 9 | Did the patient have a similar reaction to the same or similar drugs in any previous exposure? | Unknown | 0 |
| 10 | Was the adverse event confirmed by objective evidence? | Unknown | 0 |

**Preliminary total score: 3**

**Interpretation: POSSIBLE**

*Note: This is a provisional educational score based on the limited case information. The first question assumes established reports of amoxicillin-associated reactions such as rash and itching. The remaining unanswered clinical questions require verification.*

## 8. Comparison of WHO-UMC and Naranjo Methods

| Feature | WHO-UMC | Naranjo Algorithm |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment type | Qualitative | Quantitative |
| Method | Clinical judgement | Ten-question questionnaire |
| Output | Six causality categories | Four probability categories |
| Numerical score | No | Yes |
| Application | Clinical causality assessment | Structured ADR probability assessment |

Both methods help assess suspected drug-related reactions, but neither independently proves that a medicine caused an adverse event.

## 9. Final Conclusion

Based on the available patient information, amoxicillin is identified as the leading suspected medication associated with the reported rash, itching, nausea and dizziness.

The temporal relationship and known adverse reaction profile support further evaluation of amoxicillin as a possible causative drug.

The preliminary WHO-UMC classification is **Possible**, and the provisional Naranjo score is **3 (Possible)**.

Additional clinical information, including dechallenge, previous exposure, alternative causes and objective findings, is required for a more complete causality assessment.
